# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Bioinformaticians, clinical geneticists and wet-lab researchers who have a question and some data (a VCF, a counts matrix, an .h5ad, a PDF report) and want an analysis, not a chat. They work at a desk, often for long sessions, switching between Attma, papers and their own notebooks, sometimes dictating in their first language.

## Product Purpose

Attma is a conversational bioinformatics workbench: the user describes an analysis or attaches data, and an agent on Meta's Muse models plans the workflow, runs each step against real sources (HPO, Ensembl VEP, UniProt, AlphaFold, PubMed, g:Profiler, a Python sandbox, AlphaGenome) and reports what the data show with the source of every claim. Success is a researcher trusting the result enough to cite it, and reproducing it from the steps shown.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, alive. The voice is a senior colleague who shows the work: short sentences, numbers with units, sources inline, caveats stated plainly. The interface stays quiet so results (figures, tables, step timelines) carry the color.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI chat wrappers: a centered bubble chat with a gradient logo and nothing that shows work.
- AI-generated medical advice apps: no diagnosis language, no patient-facing tone.
- Neon "sci-fi lab" dark themes and glowing DNA clip art.
- Dashboards as the product: metrics tiles and charts for their own sake.

## Design Principles

1. Show the work. Every answer exposes its steps, the tool or model that ran each one, and the sources behind it.
2. Results carry the color. Chrome is neutral and recedes; the mint accent marks actions, state and Muse-run steps only.
3. Earned familiarity. Standard app-shell patterns (side nav, composer, menus) behave exactly as researchers expect from the best tools.
4. Reproducible by default. Code, inputs and outputs of each step stay one click away.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: body text at least 4.5:1, large text and UI boundaries at least 3:1, in both themes. Respect reduced motion. Dictation in 25 languages; never rely on color alone for state (icons and labels accompany it).
